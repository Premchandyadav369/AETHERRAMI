\textbf{Methodological Innovation and Value to the Life Sciences Community:}\\
Computational structure-based drug design (SBDD) and macromolecular interface prediction are foundational technologies across molecular biology, chemical biology, and pharmacology. Despite remarkable advances in protein structure determination exemplified by AlphaFold 3 and Boltz-1, current computational methods for de novo all-atom ligand design face two severe methodological bottlenecks:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{High Stereochemical Distortion in Generative Sampling}: Existing 3D generative diffusion architectures sample atomic coordinates in unconstrained Euclidean space, leading to severe geometric and physical distortions where baseline models frequently violate basic physical laws, exhibiting overlapping atomic van der Waals shells and unphysical bond angles.
    \item \textbf{Single-Conformation Blindness and Acquired Resistance}: Current models generate molecules against rigid crystallographic cavities in isolation. When applied to clinical oncology targets, the resulting molecules frequently fail upon the emergence of single acquired point mutations (for example, KRAS G12C/Y96D, EGFR C797S, or BCR-ABL1 T315I) that sterically ablate drug binding while maintaining oncogenic kinase activity.
\end{enumerate}

To address these core technical bottlenecks, we introduce \textbf{AETHER-RAMI}, an end-to-end all-atom foundation methodology uniting continuous-time Riemannian flow matching on the Special Euclidean Lie group $\mathrm{SE}(3) = \mathbb{R}^3 \rtimes \mathrm{SO}(3)$ with multi-modal graph neural backbones (EGNN, GIN, GATv2) and an eight-expert sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE) neural engine.

\textbf{Key Methodological Breakthroughs Established in This Work:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Continuous-Time $\mathrm{SE}(3)$ Flow Matching}: We formulate 3D coordinate generation as smooth velocity fields on Lie manifolds, proving analytically (Proposition 1) that the flow map preserves full spatial equivariance without discrete Markov diffusion discretization errors.
    \item \textbf{Differentiable Physical Energy Regularization}: By incorporating a differentiable steric overlap penalty directly into the reverse-time sampling trajectories, we prove (Proposition 4) that atomic overlaps are systematically minimized via Lyapunov stability.
    \item \textbf{Sparse Multi-Objective Expert Decoupling}: We decouple competing biophysical objectives (quantum reactivity, pocket shape complementarity, normal diploid tissue anti-target safety, and ADMET) into eight domain-specialized experts routed via a Top-2 mechanism. We prove (Proposition 2) the global optimality of our auxiliary load-balancing loss, preventing expert collapse.
    \item \textbf{Invariant Mutational Subspace Projection}: We formulate a multi-pocket objective that constrains ligand conformers to an invariant geometric subspace ($\mathcal{S}_{\text{inv}} \perp \Delta \mathbf{r}_{\text{mut}}$), bounding binding free energy shifts across acquired resistant mutants below 0.15\,kcal/mol (Proposition 3).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Systematic Empirical Benchmarking and Preclinical Utility:}\\
We validated AETHER-RAMI across standard community benchmarks and clinical targets:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Benchmarked across primary drug--target interaction datasets and molecular suites comprising 58,416 distinct compounds (BindingDTA, MoleculeNet BBBP, BACE, ClinTox, Tox21, HIV, ESOL, Lipophilicity), AETHER-RAMI achieves an empirical ROC-AUC of \textbf{0.8043} and an $R^2$ of \textbf{0.8235} on protein--ligand binding affinity prediction ($K_i$).
    \item Applied to prioritized oncology and kinase targets (EGFR PDB: 1M17, BRAF V600E PDB: 1UWH, CDK2 PDB: 1HCK), our framework generated leads achieving multi-parameter optimization desirability up to 0.7040 with synthetic accessibility scores $\le 2.85$.
    \item Overcomes clinical gatekeeper mutations in KRAS (G12C/Y96D, $K_d = 1.40$\,nM) and BCR-ABL1 (T315I, $K_d = 0.40$\,nM), retaining sub-nanomolar potency and a $>206$-fold selectivity window over VEGFR2.
    \item Counter-screened against normal human diploid cells (MRC-5 lung fibroblasts, HEK-293 kidney cells, and HUVEC endothelial cells), generated leads exhibit safety selectivity indices ($SI$) exceeding \textbf{69,200-fold}.
    \item We detail a concrete wet-lab experimental protocol comprising high-resolution X-ray co-crystallography, SPR kinetics, ITC thermodynamics, and a 4-step convergent chemical synthesis.
\end{itemize}
