🧠 Complete Multimodal Deep Learning Discovery Architecture

AETHER-RAMI combines evolutionary biological pre-training (ESM-2), molecular graph contrastive learning (Graph-CL), SE(3)-equivariant geometric neural networks (EGNN), conditioned 3D diffusion, and reinforcement learning (PPO) reward optimization into a closed-loop biopharmaceutical engine:

1. ESM-2 Protein Language Modeling

ESM-2 is trained over millions of evolutionary protein sequences using masked language modeling. Unlike static AlphaFold/PDB crystal coordinates, ESM-2 captures evolutionary co-variation, dynamic flexibility, and cryptic allosteric pockets. Each target residue is mapped to a rich 1280-dimensional contextual feature vector capturing hydrogen bonding potential and catalytic propensity.

2. Graph Contrastive Learning (Graph-CL)

Trained on 2.8 million bioactive molecules from ChEMBL and PubChem. Using stochastic data augmentations (Node Dropping, Subgraph Masking, and Bond Perturbation) with Normalized Temperature-scaled Cross Entropy (NT-Xent) loss, the GNN learns generalized pharmacophore manifolds that prevent the model from getting stuck in narrow, un-synthesizable chemical space.

3. SE(3)-Equivariant GNNs (EGNN)

Guarantees rotational and translational invariance in 3D Euclidean space: if the protein pocket is rotated in 3D space, the predicted ligand pose rotates identically without coordinate distortion. Inter-atomic pairwise distances update invariant node features and directional coordinate vectors concurrently.

4. Target-Conditioned 3D Diffusion

Continuous reverse diffusion denoises atom coordinates and categorical atom types simultaneously within the target pocket boundary. Cross-attention layers steer the diffusion trajectories toward the receptor's electrostatic field, ensuring perfect shape and charge complementarity.

🎯 Pharmacology Metric Directionality & Optimization Objectives

In biopharmaceutical discovery, different computational metrics have distinct directional optimization goals. Below is the master directional guide explaining which parameters must be minimized, maximized, or kept strictly within calibrated physiological windows:

⬇️ LOWER IS BETTER (Minimize)

Docking Score / Binding Free Energy (ΔG)
Optimal: ≤ -10.0 kcal/mol (More negative = Better)

Thermodynamic free energy of complexation. A more negative value indicates spontaneous, tight non-covalent bond formation and displacement of pocket water molecules.

Dissociation Constant (Kd / Ki / IC50)
Optimal: < 10 nM (Sub-nanomolar = High Potency)

Lower concentration required to inhibit 50% of enzyme activity. Sub-10 nM leads enable low daily clinical doses, reducing off-target organ toxicities.

Synthetic Accessibility (SA Score)
Optimal: 1.0 - 3.5 (Scale 1 to 10)

Estimates organic synthetic feasibility based on available reaction transforms. Lower scores ensure organic bench chemists can physically synthesize the compound in fewer steps.

PoseBusters Steric Clash Violations
Optimal: 0 Clashes (Zero Atom Overlap)

Measures unphysical overlapping van der Waals radii between ligand atoms and receptor residues. Any steric clash indicates an impossible physical pose.

Lipinski Rule of 5 Violations
Optimal: 0 Violations (Max Allowable: 1)

Violations of MW, cLogP, HBD, or HBA. Zero violations ensure optimal passive gastrointestinal absorption.

⬆️ HIGHER IS BETTER (Maximize)

Quantitative Estimate of Drug-Likeness (QED)
Optimal: > 0.70 (Scale 0.0 to 1.0)

Integrated harmonic desirability function combining MW, lipophilicity, polar surface area, and chemical structural alerts into an overall drug-likeness index.

Real-World Potential Score
Optimal: ≥ 85 / 100 (Grade A+ / A)

Composite translation index integrating target binding affinity, selectivity over off-targets, synthetic feasibility (SA score), calibrated ADMET profile, and IP differentiation.

Oral Bioavailability (F%)
Optimal: > 60% Oral Exposure

Percentage of active drug reaching systemic blood circulation unchanged after oral tablet swallowing.

Human Liver Microsomal Stability (T1/2)
Optimal: > 60 minutes

Half-life under hepatic Phase-I oxidative metabolism. Higher stability prevents first-pass hepatic destruction, supporting once-daily patient dosing.

Target Isoform Selectivity Margin
Optimal: > 50-fold to > 120-fold

Ratio of off-target to on-target IC50 (e.g. BACE1 vs BACE2, JAK3 vs JAK2). Higher selectivity prevents unwanted off-target organ toxicities.

🎯 OPTIMAL WINDOW (Goldilocks Range)

Calculated Lipophilicity (cLogP)
Optimal Window: 1.5 to 3.5

Too Low (<1.0): Compound cannot permeate cell membrane lipid bilayers.
Too High (>5.0): Insoluble, precipitates, accumulates in adipose tissue, and causes liver toxicity.

Molecular Weight (MW)
Optimal Window: 250 - 480 Da (Max 650 Da)

Too Small (<200): Lacks sufficient binding contacts for high selectivity.
Too Bulky (>650): Poor permeability and low oral bioavailability.

Topological Polar Surface Area (TPSA)
Optimal: 40 - 90 Å² (CNS) | 60 - 130 Å² (Peripheral)

Too High (>140 Å²): Cannot cross blood-brain barrier (BBB) or gut epithelium.
Too Low (<30 Å²): High non-specific plasma protein binding and low aqueous solubility.

Rotatable Bonds (Flexibility)
Optimal Window: 3 to 7 Rotatable Bonds

Too Rigid (<2): Cannot adapt to induced-fit pocket conformational changes.
Too Floppy (>10): Loses large binding free energy due to entropic freezing penalty.
