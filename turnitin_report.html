The discovery, computational design, and clinical authorization of disease-modifying medicines remains a prolonged and capital-intensive process in modern biomedical science. Bringing a single new molecular entity to regulatory approval routinely requires 12 to 15 years and capital expenditures exceeding 2.6 billion USD [1]. Despite advances in deep learning for macromolecular structure determination, exemplified by AlphaFold 2, AlphaFold 3, Boltz-1, and NovaAtom-Lite, the translation of computational hits into human clinical medicines continues to experience attrition rates exceeding 90% during Phase I–III human trials.

This late-stage clinical attrition stems predominantly from three biophysical and methodological limitations in current structure-based drug design (SBDD) algorithms. First, frontline targeted precision therapies exert selective evolutionary pressure on malignant cell populations, selecting for secondary and tertiary gatekeeper mutations that sterically abolish drug binding while preserving catalytic kinase function. In non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), the covalent KRAS G12C inhibitor Sotorasib (AMG 510) triggers clinical resistance within 6 to 12 months through acquired switch-II pocket substitutions, specifically Y96D and A146T [2]. Similarly, 3rd-generation covalent EGFR inhibitors such as Osimertinib fail upon the emergence of the tertiary C797S gatekeeper mutation [3], and BCR-ABL1 tyrosine kinase inhibitors succumb to the T315I mutation [4]. Current generative architectures model isolated wild-type crystallographic structures, failing to co-optimize against mutational escape pathways. Second, computational generative models maximize binding affinity scores in silico without modeling anti-target counter-screening or cytotoxicity against normal human diploid cells. Third, systematic evaluation on the PoseBusters benchmark demonstrates that over 45% of molecules generated by baseline models violate basic physical laws, exhibiting overlapping atomic van der Waals radii, strained bond angles, and warped aromatic rings [5].

For prospective wet-lab validation, recombinant target proteins are expressed in Escherichia coli BL21(DE3) induced with 0.4 mM IPTG at 18 °C for 16 hours, purified via Ni-NTA chromatography, and immobilized onto a Biacore Series S Sensor Chip CM5 via standard amine coupling to determine kinetic association and dissociation rates.
